How it works
  1. Open a PDF, PNG or JPG (or drop / paste it). For PDFs pick the page and resolution.
  2. Add one panel per heatmap.
  3. Grid: click the outer top-left and bottom-right corners of the heatmap, then set rows, columns and labels.
  4. Colorbar: click the two ends of the colorbar along its middle, then click at least two tick marks and type their values.
  5. Check the results (toggle Reconstruct to compare colors), then download CSV or export the project zip to reload later.
  1. Open a PDF, PNG or JPG of the figure, for example an IVIS luminescence image (or drop / paste it). For PDFs pick the page and resolution.
  2. Grid (optional): if the animals are arranged in boxes, click the outer top-left and bottom-right corners of the grid, then check rows, columns and box names.
  3. Colorbar: click the two ends of the colorbar, then click at least two ticks and type their values (e.g. 0.4e8 for 0.4 × 10⁸).
  4. Regions: draw ellipses (Shift = circle), rectangles (Shift = square) or polygons. A region drawn in one box is copied into every box; drag a copy to nudge it in that box only.
  5. Grayscale pixels (the photo) count as no signal. Read the results table, download the CSV, or export the project zip.
  1. Open a figure with a near-continuous map: a spectroscopy map, a fluorescence image with a calibration bar (or drop / paste it).
  2. Plot area: click the top-left and bottom-right corners of the image area, inside the axes.
  3. Colorbar: click the two ends of the colorbar, then click at least two ticks and type their values.
  4. Scale (optional): for images without axes, click both ends of a scale bar and type its length. Profiles can then be plotted in µm, mm, …
  5. Axes (optional): click two or more labelled ticks on each axis and type their values. Without them, coordinates are pixels.
  6. Results: bin size 1 gives one value per pixel; larger bins take the median and smooth out JPEG noise. Profiles give values along a line. Download CSVs or export the project zip.

Published images are usually compressed and contrast-adjusted: values are in the units of the colorbar as displayed, the colorbar limits how many levels can be told apart, and colors at its ends may be clipped (Show flags).

Switch tools at the top at any time: the file and both tools' work stay, and one project zip holds everything. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, ← → to flip PDF pages, drag handles to adjust. Esc cancels, Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes; change the hotkeys in Settings. Everything runs in your browser; files are never uploaded, except page images sent to OpenRouter when you run the agent.
